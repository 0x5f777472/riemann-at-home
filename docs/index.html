Riemann@home: a joint statement from independent builders

We, the undersigned, each built Levent Alpöge's Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (released November 20, 2026) on our own computers, from the official archive (sha256 9631b4f0…c7fc) with the pinned Lean 4.33.1 and Mathlib.

Each signatory:

  1. compiled all 175,187 modules (lake build Main) without errors,
  2. confirmed the final theorem depends only on Lean's three standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound),
  3. exported the compiled proof and reproduced the same raw export file as other independent builders (sha256 5ea74b91…244e),
  4. where noted, re-checked that export with an independent checker written separately from Lean's own kernel (nanoda, con-leche, lean4checker or lean4lean), named beside that signature.

We do not certify the mathematics. That the statement is Mathlib's Riemann Hypothesis, and that the proved theorem is exactly that statement, was checked by the Lean and Mathlib communities (their statement). Each signature is the signer's own report, tied to their GitHub account.

We join the Lean and Mathlib communities in congratulating Levent Alpöge.

The Riemann Hypothesis is one of math’s most famous unsolved problems: a 160-year-old guess about where prime numbers fall, with a $1 million prize for proving it. On November 20, 2026, Levent Alpöge published a proof, written with help from AI in Lean, a language where a computer checks every step. The proof is so big (175,187 files) that checking it takes about two days on a fast computer, and many people independently running that check is one of the four reasons the Lean and Mathlib communities give in their statement certifying the proof. This site tracks everyone who runs that check on their own machine, because the more people who independently get the same result, the more we can trust it.
